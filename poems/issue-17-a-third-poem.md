# A third poem

I will fill these pages with a thousand words of poetry,
a sprawling canvas stretched across the white expanse of silence,
where every syllable is a brushstroke dipped in something living,
and every line a road that leads you somewhere you have never been.

I have been thinking about the way the morning comes so slowly,
how darkness does not vanish but instead begins to thin and loosen,
the way a fist might open finger by reluctant finger,
releasing all the hours it had clutched throughout the sleeping.

The birds are first to notice, as they always are, the shifting,
they read the light the way the old ones read the bones of creatures,
and they begin their calling long before the rest have wakened,
a chorus built from instinct, from the marrow of existence.

I want to write about the things that do not make the headlines,
the cup of tea gone cold beside a window in November,
the way a child will stop mid-run to watch a beetle passing,
the whole world paused a moment in the gap between two heartbeats.

We are always rushing past the very thing we came here for,
our shoes upon the pavement beating out a frantic rhythm,
our eyes fixed on the middle distance, somewhere past the present,
some future we are certain will be better than this moment.

But what if it is not? What if the gift was always given
and we kept returning it, insisting on a different color,
what if grace has been distributed equally and freely
and we are standing in it like a man who cannot swim who drowns in shallow water?

I think of all the hands I have been given and not taken,
the doors held open, weathered, by a stranger in the winter,
the letters never written that were burning in my fingers,
the words I swallowed down like stones that settled in my stomach.

There is a kind of courage in the ordinary moment,
in choosing to be present when your mind would rather wander,
in looking at the person sitting with you at the table
as though they are the last one and the first one all together.

The autumn does this beautifully, it makes you pay attention,
it burns the trees like torches just before they all go naked,
it says look here, look now, look close, before the grey comes creeping,
before the cold lays everything to rest beneath the quiet.

I grew up watching seasons in a town with no distinction,
where summer bled to winter with a kind of gray indifference,
but I have lived in places where the fall arrives like theater,
and I have stood slack-jawed upon the stage and wept without a reason.

Beauty does that sometimes, breaks you open without warning,
a piece of music floating from a window in the evening,
the particular way the golden hour falls across a kitchen
where someone who you love is doing something very simple.

These are the things worth writing, worth the ink and the attention,
not the grand events that fill the books they keep in history,
but the private revolutions, the interior upheavals,
the moment that you know you are becoming someone different.

I have been different many times and lost the older versions,
I have mourned them some and celebrated some and barely noticed,
the self is like a river, always moving through its changes,
and the water that you step in is not there when you step out again.

The Greeks knew this and worried, drew their circles in the margin,
debated over personhood and what it meant to be it,
but I think the self is less a thing and more a kind of tending,
a garden that requires your hands returning every morning.

You are the sum of all the things you choose to keep attending,
the thoughts you feed, the voices that you sit with at your table,
the questions that you carry and the ones you set beside the road,
you are the pilgrim and the path and everything between them.

I want to write about the ocean but I fear it has been written,
every metaphor exhausted, every shore already taken,
and yet the ocean does not seem to mind its overuse in poetry,
it simply keeps on being what it is without revision.

Maybe that is something we could learn from all that water,
to be so fully what we are that imitation honors,
to be so vast and various and constantly in motion
that anyone who tries to catch us only catches one small moment.

The moon is out there pulling, as it always has been pulling,
and we are mostly water, mostly tidal, mostly turning,
responding to the forces that we cannot see or name well,
and rising in the darkness before falling back to resting.

I have tried to write a thousand words of something worth the reading,
and I am not sure I have managed, not sure anyone can manage,
to make a thousand words mean more than silence might have offered,
to justify the breaking of the beautiful and empty.

And yet here we are together, you and I, improbably,
your eyes upon these lines I made from nothing on a Tuesday,
or a Friday, or whatever ordinary day I found these,
two strangers briefly touching through the membrane of the language.

That is the only miracle that I have ever witnessed,
not the loaves, not the water into wine, not any healing,
but this, that we can reach across the absolute of difference,
and know each other, dimly, in the space between the symbols.

So I give you these thousand words like someone gives a blanket,
not a perfect gift, not what you needed, but the best I carried,
something woven out of everything I gathered from the living,
and I hope it keeps you warm at least the length of the reading.
