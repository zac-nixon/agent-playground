# A third poem

A thousand words is what you ask of me,
A poem stretching wide as open sea,
So let me fill the page with something true,
A thousand words composed just here for you.

I'll start with morning, when the light first breaks
Across the hills and every creature wakes,
The birds begin their chorus in the trees,
The flowers slowly open in the breeze,
The dew sits fresh upon the blades of grass,
And night retreats like shadows made of glass,
The sun ascends its golden throne on high,
And paints in rose and amber all the sky.

The farmer rises early from his bed,
His boots already waiting to be fed
With laces tied and purpose set in place,
He greets the dawn with weathered, knowing face,
He tends his fields before the world is stirred,
His labor quiet, rarely ever heard,
Yet from his hands the bread of life is grown,
And every loaf connects him to his own.

The city wakes in different kind of way,
With sirens, horns announcing start of day,
The coffee shops are filling up their floors,
With workers pushing through the swinging doors,
The trains are packed with faces looking down,
At glowing screens that map out modern town,
The suits and ties and sneakers on the street,
The thousands of our separate lives that meet.

A child is walking slowly off to school,
Her backpack heavy, trying to be cool,
She watches for a puddle she might jump,
She navigates the sidewalk's every bump,
She thinks of recess, thinks of her best friend,
She wonders when the school day is to end,
And yet inside that classroom she will find,
The keys that slowly open up her mind.

An old man sits upon a wooden bench,
His memories a never-ending trench
Of decades deep with love and loss and joy,
He thinks back to the time when he was boy,
He sees his mother standing at the door,
He hears his father's voice forevermore,
He feels the grip of time upon his hands,
And knows the way that every life expands.

Then contracts back to something small and still,
The way a river yields unto the hill,
The way a candle burning long must fade,
And every debt of living must be paid,
Yet he does not sit heavy with his grief,
For in the seasons he has found relief,
The spring returns no matter winter's length,
And age has given him a different strength.

The ocean calls to those who love the shore,
Its restless voice repeating evermore,
The waves that curl and crash and pull away,
Have been performing this eternal play
Since long before the first of us arrived,
Before our cities rose and then contrived
To name the sea and chart its every mile,
It rolled beneath the stars in its own style.

I think of all the sailors lost at sea,
Who gave their lives for something wild and free,
Who chased the horizon to its farthest reach,
And found the world was never out of teach,
The Portuguese, the Vikings, and the rest,
Who pointed wooden ships toward the west,
And fell off no edge as the fearful said,
But found instead new worlds stretched out ahead.

Now let me turn to love, for what is verse
Without the sweetest topic and the curse
Of every poet picking up a pen,
Who writes of it and drops the quill and then
Returns to write again with bleeding heart,
For love is both the wound and healing art,
It lifts us to the highest mountain peak,
And leaves us trembling, breathless, pale and weak.

To love another person fully known,
With every flaw and fracture fully shown,
To choose them in the morning and the night,
To hold them in the ordinary light,
Not only when the world is bright and clear,
But in the fog and in the doubt and fear,
This is the work that living asks of us,
To build something of meaning from the dust.

And friendship, that less celebrated bond,
Of which the greatest poets grow less fond
To write about, yet it sustains us well,
Through every kind of private personal hell,
The friend who calls you when you cannot call,
Who catches you before and after fall,
Who laughs with you at nothing until two,
There is no better gift the world gives you.

Now let me speak of nature once again,
The forests and the mountains and the plain,
The deserts burning golden in the heat,
The arctic tundra cold beneath your feet,
The jungle thick with life beyond all count,
The clear cold water flowing from the mount,
This world is so extravagantly made,
That beauty comes in every shape and shade.

We are so lucky, even when we grieve,
To live among the wonders and believe
That something in the structure of the whole,
Has room for and has need of every soul,
That we are not an accident of space,
But something meant to be here in this place,
Or if we are an accident, how grand,
How improbably beautiful and planned.

So here we are approaching something near,
The thousand words you asked for drawing near,
I've wandered through the morning and the night,
Through old men, oceans, children, love, and light,
Through farmers, cities, sailors, friends, and more,
I've knocked upon so many different doors,
And found behind each one a world as wide,
As any universe that lives inside.

A thousand words is just a start, I find,
There is no limit to what fills the mind,
No end to what the heart might want to say,
No final poem, no last and perfect day,
Just word by word we make our meaning here,
And word by word the world becomes more clear,
So take these thousand words and hold them well,
For they are yours, the story yours to tell.
