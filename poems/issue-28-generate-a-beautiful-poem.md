# Generate a beautiful poem

Before the dawn has named the sky,
the oak stands still and lets light by,
its old bark silver, cold, and wet,
holding every hour it has met.

The blossoms open one by one
like small surrenders to the sun,
and bees move through them, heavy, slow,
part of some agreement we don't know.

The swallows cut the morning air
in lines too quick for thought to snare,
they arc and fold and climb and wheel
like joy that cannot help but feel.

The sea keeps no account of days,
it rolls and breaks in its own phrase,
and what it says to stone and sand
no tongue has fully brought to land.

At dusk, a candle on a sill
turns the window bright and still,
and someone walking home below
looks up and softens at the glow.

The stars arrive without a sound,
they ask for nothing to be found,
they only burn and keep their place
and give the dark a kind of grace.

So beauty is no distant thing
that asks of us our reckoning,
it waits in oak and blossom, foam —
wherever you are, you are home.
