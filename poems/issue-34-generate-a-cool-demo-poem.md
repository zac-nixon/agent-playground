In the long heat of August the fig tree bends,
heavy with something between fruit and secret,
purple globes that split like old promises
to show the honey-dark interior.

A fig is patient. It waits inside its own skin
while the wasp navigates its labyrinth,
while the sun drags its slow hand across the wall,
while no one is watching.

Then it gives everything at once—
a sweetness too rich for the word sweet,
a softness that knows it is nearly over,
an ending that tastes like the middle of summer.

Eat it standing in the garden, juice on your wrist.
Don't bring it inside. Don't explain it.
Some things are only true
where they grew.
